# Pads and Aerophone: missing drills (2026-10-13)

What teachers, exam syllabi and apps use that the app does not have yet. Builds on `finger-drumming.md` and `aerophone.md` and does not repeat them. "Proposal" means my number or rule, not a published one. "Thin" means one source, a forum, a vendor blog or a search summary I could not open in full.

## Pads (finger drumming)

### Evidence by drill

**Velocity accuracy (hit a target level, crescendo, even taps).** Consensus: strong among drum teachers, weak among finger-drumming apps.
- Drum teaching uses fixed dynamic levels (stick heights pp to ff), ascending height ladders, and two-height tap/accent work. The numbers vary by program ([Matt Dudley, stick heights](https://mattdudleydrumming.com/2015/08/03/stick-heights-dynamics-exercise/), [Colts battery packet](https://colts.org/documents/CC25-percussion-packet-battery.pdf), [BU warm-up](https://www.bu.edu/bands/files/2021/06/Warm-Up-Sequence-Updated-6.24.21.pdf)).
- Exams test it. Trinity Rock & Pop drums Grade 3 technical focus includes a "double-stopped crescendo" ([Trinity G3](https://sogtrinityrock.trinitycollege.co.uk/instruments/drums/grade3)). Rockschool technical exercises include dynamic control (thin, third-party guide via search).
- Pads: MPC users drill velocity with Full Level off. Drills include random hits on the top 4 to 8 pads at changing strength, and a voice sample counting "one" to "four" on four velocity layers so you hear how hard you hit (thin, [MPC forum](https://www.mpc-forums.com/viewtopic.php?p=16817), search summary, thread not opened). Pads differ: one MPC One owner could not play soft hits evenly (same forum source). So calibrate per device.
- Melodics scores timing and streaks only. Its scoring article does not mention velocity ([Melodics scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring)). This is a gap the app can fill.
- Research: experts show lower striking-velocity variability than amateurs (Buck et al. 2021, *Motor Control*, acoustic, one hand; via search summary, thin). No published velocity targets for pads.
- The app has accent/ghost bands. Missing: a named target level (for example 4 or 8 bands), crescendo or decrescendo across a roll, and steadiness of unaccented strokes.

**Fills and landing on the one.** Consensus: strong.
- Fills come at the end of a 4 or 8 bar phrase. The hard part is placing the fill and landing on the next downbeat. Students rush ([Bax-Shop](https://www.bax-shop.co.uk/blog/drums/drum-fills-for-beginners/), [Stewart Hoffman, two-beat fills](https://stewarthoffmanmusic.com/practising-two-beat-drum-fills/)).
- Rockschool Debut fill test: a 3-bar groove, then a fill the examiner picks, played to the backing track at ♩=80 (thin, [Debut sample](https://www.scribd.com/document/539893542/drums-debut-2018-sample) via search).
- Mad Zach's paid tutorial has a fills lesson because "a routine can get pretty stagnant if it just loops" (search summary of the [DJTT tutorial](https://store.djtechtools.com/products/mad-zach-finger-drumming-tutorial)). Quest for Groove has a "building fills" course that splits a hand pattern over the kit ([QFG forum](https://questforgroove.discourse.group/t/finger-drumming-specific-lessons/833)).

**Playback by ear (call and response).** Consensus: strong in drum exams, rare in pad apps.
- Rockschool ear tests, Debut to Grade 8: fill playback and groove recall, played back from memory ([Rockschool companion guide](https://www.turramusic.com.au/rockschool-drums-companion-guide), [North London Drums](https://northlondondrums.co.uk/exams/rockschool-drum-exams-spring-2016-results/)).
- Trinity session skills: "a series of short drum grooves and fills, which candidates repeat back in turn, over a backing track" ([Trinity G3](https://sogtrinityrock.trinitycollege.co.uk/instruments/drums/grade3)).
- Complete Rhythm Trainer has rhythm imitation drills among 252 drills ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1550799056)).

**Improvising over a loop, with recording.** Consensus: strong.
- Trinity's alternative to playback is improvising in a given style over an unheard looped backing ([Trinity G3](https://sogtrinityrock.trinitycollege.co.uk/instruments/drums/grade3)).
- Melodics Playground mode: free play over a lesson after passing it, then record and listen back. Melodics support says Pads are included, without the keys-only scale guides (support page via search summary; [MusicTech, 2019](https://musictech.com/news/gear/melodics-playground-mode/)).
- Mad Zach says he jams constantly rather than doing finger exercises (CDM interview, via search: [CDM](https://cdm.link/mad-zach-tips-finger-drumming-production-free-live-pack/)).
- Kroker: record yourself against Maschine playback and check you were on time ([NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-master-the-art-of-finger-drumming-with-tim-kroker/)).
- Maschine Input Quantize (None / Record / Play-Rec) and 50% quantize to keep feel ([NI community, manual quote](https://community.native-instruments.com/discussion/comment/51332)).

**Independence (one hand holds an ostinato, the other varies).** Consensus: strong.
- Kroker: the right hand keeps a groove on pads 1 to 3 while the left fills sixteenths, and the right-hand groove "always stays the same" ([NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-master-the-art-of-finger-drumming-with-tim-kroker/)). Mad Zach splits core drums on the dominant hand from pads/bass on the other ([DJTT 2016](https://djtechtools.com/2016/07/20/mad-zachs-techniques-learning-fingerdrumming-routine)).
- Kit method: Ted Reed's *Syncopation*, with the reading line over a fixed ride/hat/kick ostinato ([Music Teacher](https://musicteachermagazine.co.uk/content/features/ted-reeds-syncopation-interpretations-for-the-modern-drummer), [Cruise Ship Drummer](https://cruiseshipdrummer.com/?p=2559)).
- The app has a fixed-hats, varying-kick pattern only inside grooves (per the existing notes). Missing: a reading line generated against a held ostinato.

**Rudiment to groove (orchestration).** Consensus: strong.
- Paradiddle with the right hand on hats and the left on snare, kick added under right-hand accents ([Mixdown](https://mixdownmag.com.au/features/columns/more-paradiddle-applications/)). Move the first note of each paradiddle around the kit ([Free Drum Lessons](https://freedrumlessons.com/drum-lessons/single-paradiddle-around-the-drum-set.php)).
- Quest for Groove's main method: learn stickings, then spread them over the kit ([QFG forum](https://questforgroove.discourse.group/t/finger-drumming-specific-lessons/833)).

**Chops (loop slices on pads).** Consensus: medium.
- Kroker's "loop on the fly": slice a 1-bar loop into 16 pads, replay it in order and tempo, then reorder ([NI blog](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-master-the-art-of-finger-drumming-with-tim-kroker/)). The Melodics DATSUNN lesson is described as finger drumming plus sample-chopping ([Melodics Push](https://melodics.com/partners/ableton/push/learn)).
- No AraabMuzik or Dilla practice routine found (thin). Melodics pad content is rhythm-focused. Chords and melodies sit in its keys track ([Melodics](https://melodics.com/finger-drumming)).

**Pad-layout memory and reading a pad chart.** Consensus: medium.
- Mad Zach: study the "where sounds go" diagram ([DJTT 2012](https://djtechtools.com/2012/09/03/how-to-practice-finger-drumming-and-controllerism)). Rockschool sight reading, Debut to Grade 5, on a short snare part ([North London Drums](https://northlondondrums.co.uk/exams/rockschool-drum-exams-spring-2016-results/)).
- QFG thread: read kit notation and "translate" it to the 4x4. One teacher uses pad diagrams plus left-to-right notation with colours for hands and loud/soft ([QFG](https://questforgroove.discourse.group/t/does-anyone-play-finger-drums-and-read-drum-notation/100)).

**Note repeat with pressure.** Consensus: medium, hardware-specific. With Full Level off, pressure changes the velocity of note-repeat hits on some MPCs, not on the 2000XL. Users switch rates between 1/16, 1/16T and 1/32 for hat rolls (thin, [MPC forum](https://www.mpc-forums.com/viewtopic.php?p=51958)). The browser sees only the resulting MIDI notes if the device repeats internally (inferred). The app already has trap rolls.

**Polyrhythm (3 over 4).** Consensus: medium for kit, none for pads. Count it as triplets over quarters or in 12/8. The hands play the 3 and the feet the 4. Take the click away in stages ([Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/articles/19987-rhythm-rules-polyrhythms-101), [Soundbrenner guide](https://files.soundbrenner.com/downloads/Soundbrenner_ultimate_rhythm_guide.pdf)). Complete Rhythm Trainer has a polyrhythm section (ratios not listed).

**Odd meters.** Consensus: medium for kit, none found for pads. Group 7/8 as 2-2-3, count aloud, put the click on other beats ([Drum Ninja](https://thedrumninja.com/odd-time-drumming-7-8/), [TalkBass](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/odd-time-and-the-metronome.497700/)).

**Endurance and speed bursts.** Consensus: medium, mostly one vendor. Drumometer: count strokes in 10 s at 120 BPM sixteenths, where a perfect count is 80. Go from 10 s to 90 s, and the count should scale with time. Johnny Rabb's test: the 2 s count should be double the 1 s count ([Drumometer](https://www.drumometer.com/education/exercises.htm)). No finger-drumming source.

### Pads: top 6 missing drills, ranked

1. **Velocity targets.** Best evidence and an open gap, since Melodics does not score velocity.
   - Measures: note-on velocity.
   - Calibrate first: the player hits softest and hardest 8 times each to set their range (needed because pads differ).
   - Drills: (a) a 4-band ladder (pp, mp, mf, ff), quarter notes on one pad, 4 per band. (b) A crescendo or decrescendo across a 1-bar sixteenth roll. (c) 16 even taps.
   - Scoring (proposal): (a) a hit counts when its velocity falls in its band, and you pass at ≥ 80% in band. (b) Velocity must rise with time (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.8) and cover ≥ 50% of the calibrated range. (c) Velocity spread is SD ÷ mean, and the lower the better. Star tiers come from the player's own history, since there is no published norm.
2. **Groove, fill, land.**
   - Measures: timing per hit, the downbeat after the fill, and tempo inside the fill.
   - Drill: 3 bars of groove, then a 1-bar fill (later 2 beats, then 2 bars), then the crash and kick on the one.
   - Scoring: the existing Perfect/Good windows on every note. On top of that, the landing-note offset is shown on its own, and rushing shows as fill inter-onset intervals (IOIs) shorter than the grid (mean signed error). A pass requires the landing within the Good window.
3. **Playback (call and response).**
   - Measures: pad and onset sequence.
   - Drill: the app plays 1 to 2 bars over the loop, and the player answers in the next bars. Grow from quarter notes to sixteenths, then add ghosts. This mirrors Rockschool and Trinity.
   - Scoring (proposal): match each played note to the target by pad and nearest grid slot, then score precision and recall. Pass at ≥ 90% of notes matched within the Good window, with no extra notes on strong beats.
4. **Ostinato and reading.**
   - Measures: which pads belong to which hand (from the layout).
   - Drill: the hats-and-kick ostinato loops, and a generated *Syncopation*-style line appears for the snare pad, one bar at a time.
   - Scoring: two numbers, ostinato accuracy and reading accuracy. Fail if the ostinato's accuracy drops more than a set margin below the player's ostinato-alone baseline (proposal: 10 points). That drop is the independence signal.
5. **Rudiment orchestration.**
   - Measures: the pad sequence against the sticking.
   - Drill: a paradiddle on hats and snare, then kick under the right-hand accents, then the first note moved to toms. Reuse the existing rudiment engine with a pad map per stroke.
   - Scoring: correct pad per stroke plus the existing timing and accent bands.
6. **Improvise over a loop, recorded.**
   - Measures: timing only. There are no "right notes".
   - Drill: 8 or 16 bars over a backing loop, then playback with the hits drawn on the grid.
   - Feedback, not pass/fail: mean and SD of grid offset, swing %, velocity range used, pads used, and density per bar. Optional replay "quantised 50%" next to the raw take, the Maschine idea.

Next tier: chops on 16 slices (score order and timing), pad-name memory (show "open hat", time to the correct pad), 3:4 and 7/8 grooves (the same grid engine, with new meters), and 10 s or 30 s bursts (the count against grid-perfect, as on the Drumometer).

## Aerophone (wind controller, sax pedagogy where it transfers)

### Evidence by drill

**Scales and arpeggios in all keys, at tempo.** Consensus: strong.
- Exam syllabi set keys, range, articulation and minimum speed. New Zealand Music Examinations Board (NZMEB) saxophone:
  - Grade 1: G, F, B♭ major plus two minors, one octave, all tongued or all slurred, minimum ♩=100 at one note per beat ([NZMEB G1](https://www.nzmeb.org/syllabuses/performance-saxophone/grade1)).
  - Grade 3: 4 major and 4 minor keys, some over 2 octaves, ♩=72 at two notes per beat ([NZMEB G3](https://www.nzmeb.org/syllabuses/performance-saxophone/grade3)).
  - Grade 5: scales and arpeggios at ♩=66, four notes per beat, plus scales in thirds at 60 ([NZMEB G5](https://www.nzmeb.org/syllabuses/performance-saxophone/grade5)).
- Klosé's *25 Daily Exercises*: the publisher suggests playing them at different speeds and articulations, in all 12 keys, and "without the octave keys" ([Stretta](https://www.stretta-music.com/25-exercises-journaliers-nr-101318.html)).
- Evan Tate's *The Book of EWI* covers major, minor, blues, pentatonic, whole-tone and diminished scale exercises plus a trill chart (table of contents via search, thin). EWI Trainer (iOS) does "any scale type and root note" with a fingering diagram ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ar/app/ewi-trainer/id6743449690)).
- Roland's lessons stop at six major keys, untimed ([KB 4414721094811](https://support.roland.com/hc/en-us/articles/4414721094811)).

**Articulation mixes.**
- NZMEB Grade 5 requires "all tongued/all slurred/two slurred, two tongued" ([G5](https://www.nzmeb.org/syllabuses/performance-saxophone/grade5)).
- A UK teacher's woodwind scale series runs: tongued up/slurred down, legato, staccato, tongue 2 slur 2, slur 2 tongue 2, slur 3 tongue 1 ([TES](https://www.tes.com/en-us/teaching-resource/scales-and-arpeggio-bundle-for-woodwind-instrumental-teaching-13307865)).
- Repeated-note scales (twos, threes, fours) for tonguing speed ([Music Mark](https://www.musicmark.org.uk/a-common-approach/woodwind/area-b/pos-4/b5/)).
- The app has staccato vs legato length only.

**Sustained note with a dynamic shape, and a breathing plan.** Consensus: strong.
- NZMEB sustained note:
  - Grade 1: 6 s at mf.
  - Grade 3: 8 to 10 s, p to f to p.
  - Grade 5: 10 to 12 s, p to f to p.
- NZMEB sustained scale: each note 2 beats at ♩=60, tongued, "Breathe every 8 beats or 4 notes" (G3, G5).
- EWI dynamic range "from barely audible, to lifting the roof off" ([Sax School Online](https://saxschoolonline.com/articles/ewi-tips-its-all-about-the-w/)).
- *Messa di voce* ideal is a symmetric triangle. Singers with large ranges shorten the decrescendo ([Buffalo abstract](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/messa-di-voce-an-investigation-of-the-symmetry-of-crescendo-and-d/)).
- The app has long tone and swell. Missing: named target levels (pp to ff steps), dynamics carried across changing notes, and a phrase with breath marks.

**Register and octave-key crossings.** Consensus: strong that it is the hard part.
- Patchman: the left-thumb octave system is "the biggest hurdle" and takes "a few months" ([Patchman FAQ](https://www.patchmanmusic.com/WindControllerFAQ.html)).
- Sax top C♯ to D, where many fingers move at once, is drilled silently and slowly. One lesson builds five exercises up to a 2-octave D major scale ([MusicGurus](https://www.musicgurus.com/courses/introducing-the-saxophone-part-2/lessons/top-c-sharp-and-d-fingering-and-exercises), [Cafe Saxophone](https://cafesaxophone.com/threads/how-to-play-c-to-d-quickly.5724/)).
- Roland has one octave-key lesson (G A B C D).

**Trills and finger-pair speed.**
- Book of EWI trill chart (thin).
- Klosé and Rascher's *158 Exercises* train evenness. Trill-fingering tips for problem pairs (low C♯ to D, G♯ to A) ([The Instrumentalist](https://theinstrumentalist.com/august-september-2020/saxophone-trill-fingerings/)).
- Speed only once even ([Taming the Saxophone](https://tamingthesaxophone.com/lessons/exercises/dexterity)).
- The tempo-ramp trill (60 to 160 BPM over 10 min) is a bass source, so it is not wind-specific ([No Treble](https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2013/09/09/finger-stamina-exercises-for-bassists/)).

**Vibrato with the bite.** Consensus: strong on sax rates, thin on wind controllers.
- Teal: four pulses per beat at 80 to 84 BPM (320 to 336 per min) as the 1963 "going rate". A player should manage 240 to 360 per min without fatigue. Luckey: 300 to 400 per min for soprano and alto, 260 to 360 for tenor and baritone. Practice with fixed pulses per beat from about 60 BPM up ([Bret Pimentel](https://bretpimentel.com/saxophone-vibrato/)).
- EWI: "gently wiggles the jaw" ([Patchman](https://www.patchmanmusic.com/WindControllerFAQ.html)). The AE-10 bite modes are in `aerophone.md`.

**Thumb lever bend to a target.** Consensus: thin.
- Vashlishan's EWI4000s method (DMA 2011) adds notation for pitch bend and glissando ([Miami](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/The-Akai-Electric-Wind-Instrument-EWI4000s/991031447575902976), abstract only).
- Horn players do pitch-bending long tones ([Horn Matters](https://www.hornmatters.com/?p=492)).
- No wind-controller bend drill found.

**By ear, play-along, sight-reading.**
- Jazz pedagogy: sing, then play, then write ([tonebase](https://resources.tonebase.co/saxophone/saxophone-posts/start-transcribing-richard-philbin-on-learning-jazz-solos-by-ear), [BetterSax](https://bettersax.com/how-to-learn-tunes-without-the-real-book/)).
- ABRSM Jazz Sax aural tests are question and answer over piano ([Forsyths](https://www.forsyths.co.uk/sheet-music/saxophone/7249-jazz-sax-aural-tests-a-quick-studies-levelsgrades-1-3-9781860963353.html)).
- Tonestro scores pitch and rhythm on 400+ songs with backing (retailer figure) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1447013289)).
- Roland GO Plus and Lesson have play-along with guide melody (in `aerophone.md`).

**Transposition.** Not a drill in any source. It is a display and setting issue, already covered in `aerophone.md`. Overtones (Rascher *Top Tones*) do not transfer, because the Aerophone has no overblowing.

### Aerophone: top 6 missing drills, ranked

1. **Timed scales and arpeggios in all 12 keys.**
   - Measures: note sequence and onsets.
   - Drill: major, natural/harmonic minor and chromatic, one octave then two (the octave key), arpeggios. Use NZMEB speeds as level anchors: ♩=100 at 1 note per beat, then ♩=72 at 2, then ♩=66 at 4.
   - Scoring: (a) every note in order; a wrong note gives the existing slip hint, and an octave error (±12) names the thumb key. (b) Onsets within the Pads timing windows. Pass at ≥ 90% Good with 0 wrong notes (proposal). Tempo ladder as on Pads.
2. **Articulation patterns on scales.**
   - Measures: tongued vs slurred, inferred from CC 2. Tongued means breath dips below the note-on threshold (or a note-off) between notes. Slurred means a new note-on with no breath dip.
   - Calibrate the threshold from the player's own tongued notes (proposal). Verify on the unit that a key change with no breath dip sends no note-off gap.
   - Patterns: all tongued, all slurred, slur 2 tongue 2, tongue 2 slur 2, slur 3 tongue 1.
   - Scoring: each note-to-note join is classed as tongued or slurred and compared with the pattern. Pass at ≥ 90% of joins correct, plus timing.
3. **Dynamic targets and sustained phrases.**
   - Measures: CC 2 against calibrated floor and max.
   - Drills: (a) dynamic steps: hold pp, p, mf, f, ff for 2 beats each. (b) The NZMEB sustained note: 6 s at mf, then 8 to 10 s and 10 to 12 s p to f to p. (c) A sustained scale with breath marks every 4 notes.
   - Scoring (proposal): (a) share of samples inside the target band, per step. (b) The existing swell shape, plus symmetry (crescendo time ÷ decrescendo time) and peak level. (c) A breath (CC 2 near zero) is allowed only at marks. A breath elsewhere, or a note-off mid-note, is a fault.
4. **Register-crossing drills.**
   - Measures: notes and transition time.
   - Pairs: C♯5 to D5 (palm keys), B4 and C5 to D5, and G4 to G5 with octave keys. Loop each pair slurred at tempo. Practise silently first with Hold Mode = Key (in `aerophone.md`).
   - Scoring: correct target note, count of intermediate glitch notes (proposal: notes shorter than ~30 ms between the two targets; check against Key Delay on the unit), and onset timing. Advance tempo after a clean pass.
5. **Trills and finger pairs.**
   - Measures: alternation rate and evenness.
   - Drill: pick a pair (start with the Roland 3-note groups, then problem pairs such as low C♯ to D and G♯ to A), slurred sixteenths at a tempo, then a 10 s or 30 s timed burst.
   - Scoring: the coefficient of variation (CV) of inter-onset intervals as evenness, wrong or glitch notes, and notes per second. Raise the tempo only when CV is under a threshold set from the player's own baseline (proposal).
6. **Bite vibrato at a set rate.**
   - Measures: pitch bend oscillation. Requires bite mapped to bend; check that the AE-10's Sax mode bends only down while EWND gives cyclic vibrato.
   - Drill: hold a note with 3, then 4 pulses per beat at ♩=60, rising toward 80 to 84 (Teal).
   - Scoring: rate from peak counting or zero-crossings against the target (proposal: ±10%), regularity (CV of the period), and depth. If the app makes the sound, it sets the bend range, so depth can be shown in cents. Otherwise show raw bend units.

Next tier: echo by ear (the app plays 2 to 4 notes over a drone, the player answers; score the note sequence then timing), a thumb-lever bend to a target interval and back (score landing in cents and hold time, with thin evidence), and sight-reading songs without the fingering view.

## Caveats

- Several claims come from search summaries of pages I could not open (MPC forum, Melodics support Playground article, Rockschool guides, *The Book of EWI*). They are marked thin above.
- No source publishes pass thresholds for velocity, breath level or vibrato regularity. Every threshold above is a proposal to tune on the owner's hardware.
