# Aerophone AE-20: a saxophone curriculum adapted to it (2026-10-27)

Builds on `context/research/aerophone.md` (hardware, MIDI, Roland's Lesson app) and `context/research/pads-aerophone-gaps.md` (the six skill drills, now built). Not repeated here.

## 1. What the best sax programs do, and what transfers

### The methods and what makes them work

**Band methods (Rubank Elementary, Essential Elements).** One or two new notes a lesson, a tune from those notes straight away, keys added by flats and sharps in a fixed order. Rubank Elementary (Hovey, 1934) starts on B A G C, then works through the keyed range with major scales in C, F, G, Bb, D, Eb, A, Ab, E, selected minors, articulation studies and duets, each page a lesson repeated until clean ([Groth Music listing](https://grothmusic.com/p-9766.aspx), [Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/4470030/rubank-elementary-method-saxophone)). Essential Elements uses a rhythm-based sequence with play-along tracks ([Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/863507/essential-elements-book-1-original-series)). What works: tiny increments, immediate music, and the band book's assumption of daily repetition. Roland's Lesson app copies this order (G A B, E F G, C D E, ... Eb major).

**Larry Teal, The Art of Saxophone Playing.** The reference for American sax pedagogy. Chapters: breathing, tone, intonation, technique, attack and release, staccato, articulation, phrasing, vibrato, altissimo ([table of contents scan](https://docdrop.org/download_annotation_doc/Teal-Larry---1994---Art-of-Saxophone-Playing-Alfred-Music-b8mdz.pdf), [Wikipedia: saxophone technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxophone_technique)). The vibrato chapter is a metronome method: preliminary steps, transfer of the motion, practice patterns at fixed pulses a beat, rising to four a beat at 80 to 84 ([Bret Pimentel on Teal](https://bretpimentel.com/saxophone-vibrato/)). What works: every expressive skill is reduced to a measurable motor pattern against a click.

**Universal Method (Paul de Ville, 1908) and Klosé (25 Daily Exercises, Méthode).** Scales in every key, then the same scales in thirds, fourths, broken chords and chromatic runs, each under several articulations. Klosé's publisher suggests every exercise at several speeds and articulations, in all 12 keys ([Stretta](https://www.stretta-music.com/25-exercises-journaliers-nr-101318.html)). De Ville is public domain in the US (1908); Klosé died 1880, so his originals are public domain (the Carl Fischer edition is not). What works: one pattern, many keys and articulations, so fingers learn every key equally.

**Ferling, 48 Studies Op. 31.** Pairs of a slow lyrical study and a fast technical one in each key, the standard intermediate sax etudes ([IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/48_Studies_for_Oboe,_Op.31_(Ferling,_Franz_Wilhelm))). Public domain. What works: the slow ones train phrasing, breath planning and dynamics; the fast ones train articulation and finger evenness in context.

**Rascher, Top Tones.** Overtone exercises to build voicing and altissimo. Does not transfer: the Aerophone has no overblowing, register comes only from the thumb keys. The altissimo fingerings exist in Sax mode on the AE-20 ([Roland KB](https://support.roland.com/hc/en-us/articles/29575779757083)), so only the fingering charts are useful, and only at an advanced level.

**Lennie Niehaus, Basic Jazz Conception Vol. 1.** 12 exercises and 10 etudes on one problem: swing eighth-note phrasing (triplet feel, tongued off-beats, accents, ghosted notes), with a play-along ([Forsyths](https://www.forsyths.co.uk/music/sheet-music/instruments/woodwind/saxophone-sheet-music/112912-niehaus-lennie-basic-jazz-conception-for-saxophone-vol-1-9781934638002.html), [Thomann](https://www.thomann.co.uk/advance_music_niehaus_basic_jazz_concep_1.htm)). What works: articulation taught as the style, not decoration.

**Jamey Aebersold play-alongs.** Vol. 1 starts with blues in Bb and F, Dorian minor tracks, four-bar cadences, the cycle of dominants and ii-V7 in all keys; the book covers blues scales, pentatonics, bebop scales and patterns ([Ellis Music](https://www.ellismusic.com/p-2518.aspx), [jazzbooks.com](https://www.jazzbooks.com/jazz/product/V01DS)). What works: one chord or one form for a long time with a real rhythm section, so the player improvises from day one.

**Charlie Parker Omnibook.** Parker's heads and solos transcribed. Used for vocabulary: learn a phrase, take it through keys, use it on a blues. Parker died in 1955, so his compositions are public domain in life-plus-70 countries from 2026 (not in the US).

**Better Sax (Jay Metcalf), Sax School Online, Bob Reynolds.** The online schools converge on: fundamentals from day one ("Fast results come from building strong fundamentals on day 1", [Better Sax](https://bettersax.com/bettersax-beginner-course)), pentatonic and blues foundation courses before harmony ([Better Sax courses](https://bettersax.com/courses)), a nine-level curriculum ([Sax School Online](https://saxschoolonline.com/)) and, for the EWI, long tones, the full dynamic range and identical attacks at every volume ([Sax School EWI tips](https://saxschoolonline.com/articles/ewi-tips-its-all-about-the-w/)). Bob Reynolds puts transcription at the centre: small loopable phrases, metronome challenges, memorise tunes ([lesson archive](https://lessons.bobreynoldsmusic.com/category/transcribing/page/5), [memorise music](https://bobreynoldsmusic.com/memorize-music-in-a-hurry)). This matches the jazz literature (Berliner, *Thinking in Jazz*): imitation of recorded phrases is how jazz language is learned.

The evidence is expert consensus, not trials. The methods agree closely, which is the strongest signal available.

### Transfer to the AE-20

| Sax skill | On the AE-20 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Fingering, note order, keys | Identical in Sax mode | Full transfer: take the band-book order as is |
| Octave key, register break | Thumb octave keys; C#5 to D5 is the same finger flip | Full transfer, and the hardest part (Patchman) |
| Long tones | Breath steadiness and dynamic level on CC 2; no tone to "centre" | Partial: train the airstream and the dynamic, not the sound |
| Dynamics, crescendo, messa di voce | Breath is volume and timbre directly, and measurable | Transfers better than on sax: it can be scored |
| Tonguing, articulation | The tongue stops the air, the breath sensor sees the dip | Full transfer; already measured in the app |
| Swing articulation, ghost notes | Tongued off-beats transfer; a ghost note is a soft breath, not a half-muted reed | Mostly transfers |
| Vibrato | Bite sends pitch bend; Teal's metronome method applies | Transfers in rate and evenness; jaw vibrato habits differ |
| Intervals, scales in thirds, arpeggios | Pure finger patterns | Full transfer |
| Jazz vocabulary, transcription, blues | Notes and rhythm | Full transfer |
| Embouchure, reed, voicing, overtones, subtone, intonation by ear | Not present (pitch is fixed by the key) | Does not transfer: drop Top Tones' overtones, tuning drills, subtone |
| Altissimo | Fingerings in Sax mode, no voicing needed | Easier than sax; fingerings only |

The practical upshot: a sax curriculum minus the tone-production chapters, with the breath work reframed as dynamic control. The beginner can move faster than a sax beginner because there is no embouchure stage.

## 2. What the app has today

Seen in the app (Orca tab on `/music/aerophone`, `/music/phrases`, `/music?tab=paths`) and in code:

- **One drill page with ten modes** (`src/routes/music/aerophone/+page.svelte`, `AeroSkills.svelte`, `content/winds-pads.ts`, `content/aero-skills.ts`): Fingering (Roland's 12 lessons as flash cards, a slip named by key), Long tone (with pp to f bands), Swell, Articulation (staccato vs legato length), Interval landing (over a drone), Scales and arpeggios (6 types, 12 keys, NZMEB grade speeds, octave-slip hint), Tonguing patterns (5 groupings plus a slurred phrase, read from CC 2 dips), Register crossings (6 pairs, glitch count), Trills (6 pairs, rate and evenness), Bite vibrato (2 to 4 pulses a beat at 60 to 80). Practice and Check modes; tempo ladders via `nextRun` on scales, tonguing and register.
- **Fingering chart** Bb3 to C#6 written (`aerophone-fingerings.json`, 28 notes), 10 verified on the owner's unit. The palm-key top (D6, Eb6, E6, F6, F#6) is missing, so sax lines that reach the top of the horn cannot be shown or played in the drills.
- **Phrases**: 20 Aerophone phrases, all public domain or style vocabulary: 4 band-book tunes, classical flute and sax excerpts (Morning Mood, Badinerie, L'Arlésienne, Boléro, Bumblebee), ragtime, and 4 licks (C blues scale, E minor pentatonic, honk riff, ii-V-I, G blues lick, bebop enclosure). No pop or jazz sax songs.
- **Other drills that take the Aerophone**: Scales (generic), Jazz licks (31 licks), Note reading and Sight-reading (treble line, exact pitch), ear drills' played answers, Jam (solo over a progression), Song Studio (imports MusicXML/MIDI, Aerophone fingering row).
- **No Aerophone path.** The Paths tab lists Piano, Guitar, MPC, Ear, Jazz, Circle, Studio. Today's Aerophone slot opens the generic page (`daily-routine.json`: "Aerophone Technique", no mode).
- **No transposition.** The app treats incoming notes as written pitch. That holds only while the AE-20's transpose is C. A sax player who sets an Eb alto scene, or imports an alto part from MuseScore, gets everything off by a sixth.
- **Thresholds are proposals** (breath bands, tonguing dip 45%, trill 4 a second at 0.7 evenness, vibrato ±15%) still unverified on the AE-20 (`TASKS.md`).

Compared with the methods: the technique coverage is good, better than Roland's app (timing, breath, tonguing, vibrato scored). What is missing is the sequence (no path), the musical half (songs, swing feel, blues improvising, etudes), the top of the range, and interval patterns beyond plain scales.

## 3. Recommendations, ranked

**1. An Aerophone Path (L).** New `PathId` "aerophone" in `progress/paths.ts`, built like the MPC path's `ADDED_STEPS` (hrefs with setup in the query, `skillIds` from `aerophoneSkills`, `threshold`, `minSessions`). Order follows Rubank and Roland for notes, Teal for breath, Niehaus and Aebersold for jazz. Proposed steps (existing drill unless marked NEW):

| # | Step | Drill and setup | Done means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up and check your keys | NEW calibration (rec. 4) | Calibrated once |
| 2 | Three notes, left hand | `aerophone?mode=fingering&lesson=g-a-b`, then e-f-g, c-d-e | 3 clean Check runs |
| 3 | First tunes | `phrases`: Hot Cross Buns, Au clair, Lightly Row | 80% on each |
| 4 | Long tones at a level | `mode=long-tone&level=mf&seconds=5`, then p | 3 runs over 2 days, 80% |
| 5 | C major, then the octave key | `lesson=c-major`, `lesson=octave` | Check runs clean |
| 6 | Tongue it, slur it | `mode=articulation`, then `mode=tonguing&tongue=all-tongued` | 80% |
| 7 | F, G and Bb major, timed | `mode=scales&scale=major` at grade 1 speed (100, one a click) | Each key 90% |
| 8 | Swell (messa di voce) | `mode=swell&swellSeconds=8` | 3 runs, 75% |
| 9 | Across the break | `mode=register&pair=cs5-d5`, then c5-d5, g4-g5 | 0 glitches at 80 BPM |
| 10 | First riffs | NEW riff set, level 1 (rec. 3) | 3 riffs at tempo |
| 11 | Blues scale and the 12-bar | `phrases` blues licks, then `jam` blues in F (concert) | NEW echo step graded (rec. 6) |
| 12 | Minor keys and arpeggios | `scale=natural-minor`, `harmonic-minor`, arpeggios, grade 3 speed | 6 keys |
| 13 | Swing eighths | NEW swing articulation (rec. 5) | 80% at 120 |
| 14 | Patterns in thirds | NEW interval patterns (rec. 7) | 4 keys |
| 15 | Trills and finger speed | `mode=trills`, all six pairs | 4 a second, even |
| 16 | Bite vibrato | `mode=vibrato`, 2 then 3 then 4 a beat at 60 | 4 at 80 (Teal) |
| 17 | Jazz heads | NEW riff/head set level 2: blues heads (Sonnymoon, Tenor Madness, Now's the Time) | 3 heads |
| 18 | Read a line | Sight-reading on the Aerophone | 80% |
| 19 | All 12 keys, grade 5 speed | `mode=scales` at 66, four a click | 12 keys |
| 20 | Ferling slow study and a Parker head | Song Studio with an imported score | One performance take at 85% |

Steps 1 to 9 are a beginner's first two to three months; 10 to 20 run to early intermediate. Steps tie into Ear, Jazz and Circle paths by links, not duplication.

**2. Transposition setting and range fitting (S to M).** One setting, "Aerophone plays in C / Bb (tenor, soprano) / Eb (alto, bari)", applied to incoming MIDI (concert to written) and offered on Score import ("this part is written for Eb alto"). Song Studio should fit an imported line to the Aerophone's range by octaves, as it does for guitar and ukulele. Without this, every downloaded sax part is wrong. Source: Roland's transpose sends concert notes over USB ([KB](https://support.roland.com/hc/en-us/articles/4414507233179)).

**3. Bundle a sax riff and head set (M).** The owner has allowed popular material for this personal app; relax the public-domain rule in `phrases.ts` for an owner-only "Riffs and heads" set (the hook or head only, 2 to 8 bars). Use the list in section 4. Level them 1 to 3 like phrases; each one is a short Bob Reynolds style loop: hear, echo, then at tempo. This is the "play songs you like" goal and the strongest motivator Better Sax and Sax School both lean on.

**4. Calibration and key check (S to M).** A first-run screen: blow soft and loud to set the breath floor and ceiling (replacing the fixed 0.1 to 0.85 bands), learn which CC carries breath, finger written G4 to confirm transpose, and a "press each key" check that lights the drawing. Already recommended in the earlier research and deferred; every breath-scored step depends on it.

**5. Swing articulation drill (S to M).** Niehaus's core: swing eighths with off-beats tongued and slurred into the next beat ("doo-DAH"), accents on the off-beat, and the swing ratio scored. Fits `TONGUING` as a new group (off-beats "T", downbeats "S") plus the swing timing the Pads groove grader already measures. Run on scales, then on a blues head.

**6. Blues call and response over a backing (M).** Aebersold Vol. 1 starts improvising on the blues in week one. The app plays a 2-bar blues-scale phrase over a 12-bar backing, the player answers; later the player fills the empty 2 bars freely and is scored only on time and staying in the scale. Combines the ear "echo" drills and `jam`.

**7. Interval patterns on scales (S).** Universal Method and Klosé patterns: scales in thirds (C E D F E G...), fourths, broken triads, 1-2-3-1 groups, in the existing `AERO_SCALES` generator as a "pattern" option. Same grading and tempo ladder.

**8. Extend the chart to F#6 (S, verify on unit).** Palm keys D6, Eb6, E6, F6 and front F / side F#6 with the octave key. Many riffs (Baker Street reaches F6 on alto) need it, and Rubank covers the full keyed range by the end of book one.

**9. Phrase-level breath and dynamics (M).** Ferling's slow studies and band-book long-tone scales need breath marks and dynamic targets across changing notes. Add optional breath marks and per-bar dynamics to phrases and Song Studio, scored with the existing in-band share. Strongest place where the Aerophone can beat a real sax teacher: dynamics are measurable.

**10. Today points into the path (S).** The Aerophone slot in `daily-routine.json` opens the generic page; make it open the path's next step, or rotate long tones plus one technique mode plus one riff, sized to the session length (10 minutes: long tone and one riff; 30: add scales and a technique mode).

**11. Vibrato in music (S).** After the rate drill, apply vibrato on long notes of a slow riff (Harlem Nocturne, Careless Whisper's long notes): score that held notes over one beat carry 4 to 5 pulses a second.

Skip: overtone and voicing drills (no overblowing), intonation drills (pitch is fixed), altissimo until intermediate.

## 5. Open questions for the owner

1. Which sound and transpose do you play: an alto or tenor scene with transpose on, or C? This decides whether written-pitch grading is right today.
2. Do you want the riff set stored in the app (owner-only, as the brief allows), or only imported by you through Score import?
3. Classical (Ferling, band tunes) or pop and jazz first? The path can lead with either after step 9.
4. Is the bite set to send pitch bend (E-Wind style) for vibrato, or off as beginners are often told? The vibrato steps assume pitch bend.
5. Should the path count Check runs only, as the MPC path does, or also accept Practice with the fingering shown for the first five steps?
