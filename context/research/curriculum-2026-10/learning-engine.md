# Learning engine: how Today, mastery and paths should work

Area: learning science and the practice engine across all instruments. Builds on `context/02-research-digest.md` §(c) and `context/research/drill-progression.md`, which already cover Duke 2009, Stambaugh 2011, Carter and Grahn 2016, the guidance hypothesis, modelling, Karpicke and Roediger, and the Duolingo streak data. Those are not repeated here except where a recommendation rests on them.

## 1. What the evidence says, and what the good programs do

### Practice quantity and quality

- **Deliberate practice matters, but less than Ericsson claimed.** Platz et al. (2014) pooled 13 music studies (n = 788): r = .61 between task-relevant practice and achievement ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00646/text)). Macnamara, Hambrick and Oswald (2014, Psychological Science) put music at about 21% of variance, and Hambrick's reply argues practice explains about a third of reliable variance ([PMC4101876](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4101876)). For one adult with fixed starting talent, the lever left is the quality of each minute, which Duke, Simmons and Cash (2009) showed matters more than minutes.
- **Mastery learning works.** Kulik, Kulik and Bangert-Drowns (1990, Review of Educational Research 60(2)) found mastery-based progression (pass a criterion before moving on) lifted outcomes by about half a standard deviation ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265)). The criterion has to be real: a step that can be passed by luck teaches nothing.
- **Successive relearning beats a high first criterion.** Rawson and Dunlosky (2011, JEP: General 140(3)) found that reaching criterion once and then relearning to criterion in later sessions gave far better long-term retention than a stricter single-session criterion; extra correct recalls in the first session paid little ([doi 10.1037/a0023956](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023956)). Declarative material, but the principle maps directly onto "when is a path step done".

### Spacing, sleep and the learning versus performance gap

- **Sleep consolidates motor sequences.** Walker et al. (2002, Neuron 35) found a night of sleep gave a 20% speed gain on a finger-tapping sequence with no accuracy loss; the same hours awake gave nothing ([PubMed 12123620](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12123620/)). Simmons (2012, already in the digest) found keyboard accuracy gains only with 24-hour gaps. Duke and Davis (2006, JRME 54(2)) replicated overnight gains on keyboard sequences.
- **Gaps: too short hurts more than too long.** Cepeda et al. (2008, Psychological Science 19(11)): the best review gap is about 20 to 40% of the retention interval for a one-week horizon, falling to 5 to 10% for a year, and too-short gaps cost much more than too-long ones ([PubMed 19076480](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)). Expanding intervals follow from this.
- **In-session performance misleads.** Soderstrom and Bjork (2015) and Stambaugh (2011): blocked practice looks better on the day, interleaved or spaced practice wins at retention. A step passed in one sitting measures performance, not learning.

### Interleaving and variable practice

- **Contextual interference is real but small in applied settings.** Brady's (2004) meta-analysis: d = .57 in lab tasks, .19 in applied ones; adults show more benefit than children ([Brady 2004 PDF](https://www.gwern.net/docs/spaced-repetition/2004-brady.pdf); later meta-analyses are more cautious, [Frontiers 2024 review](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1377122/full)). Shea and Morgan (1979) is the origin. Carter and Grahn (2016) is suggestive for music, not conclusive. Conclusion: interleave within a session at the block level (different keys, variations, skills), but do not force rapid instrument switching. The digest's decision (interleaving offered, not forced) stands.
- **Variable practice** (Schmidt's schema theory; Kerr and Booth 1978) supports changing keys, tempi and voicings of one skill, which the paths already do in places (G-A2, P-A1: same lick, new key).

### Feedback, autonomy, mental practice

- **Feedback fading:** mixed (digest §c). The app's Practice and Check split already gives the useful version: guided practice with full feedback, independent checks with summary only.
- **Self-controlled practice:** McKay et al. (2022, Meta-Psychology) found the self-control learning benefit is "small and not currently distinguishable from zero" after bias correction ([Meta-Psychology](https://conferences.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2803)). Letting the owner swap blocks is worth having for adherence, not as a learning lever.
- **Mental practice** helps, less than physical: Driskell, Copper and Moran (1994, J Applied Psych 79(4)) meta-analysis d ≈ .5; Bernardi et al. (2013) found mental practice improved expert pianists' performance, to a lower degree than physical practice ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00451/text)). Useful for days with no instrument.

### Habit and motivation

- **Missing a day does not reset a habit.** Lally et al. (2010, EJSP): median 66 days to automaticity, range 18 to 254, and single missed days did not derail formation ([UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). This backs the forgiving streak already built.
- **Dropout is the main risk.** Fender's executives claim about 90% of new guitarists quit within a year ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/90-of-beginner-guitar-players-give-up-within-a-year-says-fender)); a company claim, but it is why every app here optimises for showing up.
- **Specific, proximal goals** beat "do your best" (Locke and Latham 2002); in music, Miksza's work on practice behaviours links goal-directed, self-evaluated practice to achievement ([Miksza 2007, JRME 55(4)](https://www.doi.org/10.1177/0022429408317513)).

### How the apps and teachers structure a session

| Source | Session shape | Difficulty adaptation |
|---|---|---|

| Yousician | Daily Session on Home plus a path; goal set in minutes or stars; streaks counted in weeks; 30-day challenge starts at 10 minutes, then 20 ([support](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/201558312-How-to-navigate-in-Yousician), [30-day](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017752598-30-day-playing-challenge)) | Auto speed in 5% steps |

| Simply Piano | Course path plus personalised 5-minute Workouts and a recap for returning users ([App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/ca/story/id1300124851)) | Mechanics of recap unpublished |
| Rocksmith | Song first; Riff Repeater isolates a section with speed and note-density controls; Adaptive Difficulty adds or removes notes per phrase by accuracy, with a "leveling speed" setting ([Ubisoft](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/5XfINjoRmVJgBKR8fGt4EM/rocksmith-accessibility-spotlight)) | Note density per phrase, the richest adaptation in the field |
| Duolingo Music | Very short lessons: a new note, then a song that uses it; replays what you missed ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/computing/software/duolingos-new-music-platform-will-teach-you-the-basics-of-playing-tunes)) | Path order; no published review scheduler |
| Fender Play, Flowkey, Skoove | Song-led lessons on a path; loop, slow down, wait mode | Mostly manual |

The pattern across all of them: a short default (5 to 10 minutes), a path for new material, a song as the reward, and a calendar that forgives. None publishes a real spaced-review scheduler for motor skills, and none plans across several instruments. The digest's note that "no researched app nails what to practise next" still holds.

## 2. What the app has today

Seen in the running app (Today and Paths tabs, guitar) and in code.

**Today** (`src/lib/music/today.svelte.ts`, `progress/routine.ts`, `data/daily-routine.json`)
- Budget buttons: 5, 10, 20, 30 minutes. Every block is 5 minutes; a 20-minute guitar day is spider, ear, chord work, rhythm.
- One instrument per day (`settings.activeInstrument`). Defaults per instrument: piano is scales, ear, reading, rhythm; MPC is pads, rhythm.
- Up to two weak skills (lowest accuracy, 3+ checked attempts) go first, deduplicated by step.
- No song, no improvisation, no path step and no review of due skills ever enters the set.
- The weak-spot block opens the step's route, not the skill's setup. Seen live: "Your weak spot: Fretboard Note Finder" opens `/music/fretboard?instrument=guitar...`, the spider default, not `mode=find`.
- Coach: names the weakest checked skill and lists due skills, as text only.
- Challenges: 100 XP, 3 reps on the weak spot, two capabilities, keep the streak. The first measures volume.

**Mastery** (`progress/mastery.ts`): EMA per skill, decay `exp(-0.02 × days)`, `decayRate` set once and never changed. Due when the decayed estimate falls 10 points below the last measured value.

Two consequences, worked out from the formula:
- **Strong skills come due sooner than weak ones.** A skill at 0.9 is due after 6 days, at 0.5 after 11 days, at 0.3 after 20 days, and below 0.1 never. This inverts expanding intervals (Cepeda 2008).
- **Everything halves in 35 days**, so a month away re-locks skills behind the 0.6 prerequisite threshold (`graph.isUnlocked`). A C major scale is not forgotten in a month.

**Scheduler** (`progress/scheduler.ts`): `reviewIntervalDays` already doubles the interval per observation (up to 2^8 days) scaled by mastery. That is the right shape, but only the ear drills use it (`drills/ear/session.ts`).

**Next run** (`progress/advance.ts`): two clean runs at 85% raise the tempo 5%, a run under 70% steps back, the variation drops only after two fails at the floor. Well grounded; keep it.

**Paths** (`progress/paths.ts`, `data/learning-paths.json`): seven paths (Ear, Jazz, Guitar, Piano, MPC, Circle, Studio). No Aerophone, Ukulele, Reading or Songs path. A step is done when every skill has `minAttempts` independent attempts over `minSessions` sessions with a mean credit of at least `threshold` (0.66 by default), over all history.
- **The "Done means" text is not what the code checks.** G-B2 says "90% of the strums on time at 72 BPM"; the code needs one checked attempt averaging 66% at any tempo. G-I3 "a clean run at 90 BPM", P-B1 "every pitch right at 60 BPM", M-B2 "60 ms or less at 70 BPM": none sets `minDifficulty`, a tempo, or a threshold above 0.66. Of the 26 guitar, piano and MPC table steps, most use the defaults (1 attempt, 1 session, 0.66).
- **Lifetime mean** penalises early failures forever and lets one lucky run pass a fresh step.
- **Done is permanent**: no step is ever checked again after a gap.
- **Paths do not feed Today.** The path's "next" step lives only in the Paths tab.

## 3. Recommendations, ranked

### R1. Make a path step pass on what it says, measured on a later day (M)

What: give `PathStep` structured criteria and evaluate them on recent evidence.
- Add optional `minBpm` (or reuse `minDifficulty` where the drill maps tempo to difficulty) and per-step `threshold` matching the "Done means" text. Write the text from the fields so they cannot drift.
- Judge on the last K eligible attempts (K = max(minAttempts, 3)), not the lifetime mean.
- Replace `minSessions` with `minDays` (default 2): the criterion met on one day, then the first checked attempt on a later day also passes. That is the cold check.

Why: Rawson and Dunlosky's successive relearning; Walker 2002 and Simmons 2012 on overnight consolidation; Soderstrom and Bjork on performance versus learning; Kulik 1990 on real mastery criteria.

Fit: all inside `stepEvidence` in `paths.ts` plus the JSON. Events already carry `at`, `difficulty`, `sessionId`. Add a status `"almost"` ("passed today, confirm tomorrow") so the UI says why a passed step is not yet done. Tests in `paths.test.ts`.

### R2. Fix review timing: expanding intervals instead of a flat decay (S to M)

What: make `decayRate` per skill and let it fall after each successful spaced review, rise after a failed one. A simple version: on an independent event with credit ≥ 0.85 after a gap of at least half the current interval, `decayRate *= 0.6` (floor 0.002); on credit < 0.7, `decayRate *= 1.5` (cap 0.05). Then "due" can stay "a 10-point drop", and the interval grows on its own. Alternative with less change: use the existing `reviewIntervalDays(observations, mastery)` for `isDueForReview` everywhere.

Why: Cepeda 2008 (gaps should grow, too-short is worse); the current rule reviews a 0.9 skill every 6 days forever and lets weak skills wait 20. FSRS and Anki use a per-item stability that grows on success for the same reason.

Fit: `applyEvidence` in `mastery.ts` is the only writer ("the only place mastery changes"), so this is one function plus tests. Re-locking after a break also eases. Motor skills could start at a lower base rate (0.01) than declarative ear and theory items (0.02), set by capability.

### R3. Rebuild Today as a session with a shape, flexing from 10 to 90 minutes (M)

What: replace "N five-minute blocks" with role-based blocks whose lengths scale with the budget:

| Role | What fills it | 10 min | 20 min | 45 min | 90 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Review (warm-up) | Due skills, first attempt checked (cold) | 3 | 4 | 6 | 10 |
| Focus | The path's next step or the coach's weak spot, with `advance` | 7 | 8 | 15 | 25 |
| Variety | 1 to 3 other skills, other keys or variations | | 4 | 8 | 15 |
| Song | Song Studio on the current song, trouble-spot loop | | 4 | 10 | 20 |
| Create | Jam or Phrases: improvise over a groove | | | 6 | 10 |
| Second instrument | A short focus block on another instrument | | | | 10 |

Budget buttons: 10, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90. A "less time" link mid-session drops blocks from the bottom up.

Why: Klickstein's zones (new, developing, performance, technique, musicianship); every app here leads with a short default and uses a song as the payoff; review first doubles as a retrieval check (Karpicke and Roediger) and warm-up; ending on play suits the goals of improvising and writing and leaves the session on a high. The current set has no song and no improvisation although those are two of the owner's four goals.

Fit: `buildRoutine` keeps its signature and gains `role` and variable `minutes` on `RoutineStep`. `STEPS` gains `song` (`/music/songs` or Song Studio with the last song) and `create` (`/music/jam`). The block strip in `TodayView.svelte` already sizes blocks by minutes, so it works as is. `completedSteps` already matches by `routine` and `step` params.

### R4. Feed paths and due reviews into Today (S)

What: the Focus block takes the next step of the instrument's path (`pathProgress(...).continueHref`, already computed for the Paths tab), unless the coach has a weak spot under 70%, in which case alternate by day. The Review block takes skills due under R2, ordered by `buildQueue` (already written, ear only today). Each block opens the skill's own setup: map skill to href (the weak spot "Fretboard Note Finder" should open `fretboard?mode=find`).

Why: no researched app links "what's next on the path" with "what's slipping"; that is the app's planned difference (digest §g.14). Spacing evidence for the review block as in R2.

Fit: `todayPlan` already has stats, mastery and events. `stepHref` takes an optional query override. `practiceHref` in the progress model has the same gap and can share the fix.

### R5. Plan across instruments by week, not by forcing them into one day (M)

What: a weekly split in Settings (default from the brief's priorities, for example guitar and ukulele 35%, piano 25%, Aerophone 15%, pads 10%, ear and reading 15%). Today's main instrument is the one furthest behind its share this week; the owner can still switch. Ear and reading are instrument-free and get the Review or Variety slot every day (they are declarative and perceptual, where daily spaced items have the strongest evidence). Only days of 60 minutes or more add a second instrument block. Ukulele and guitar share fretboard, chord-change and strumming skills, so the guitar path's shape-agnostic steps should accept either (`instruments: ["guitar", "ukulele"]`).

Why: spacing works over days (Cepeda, Walker); contextual interference gains are small in applied settings (Brady d = .19), so cramming four instruments into a 20-minute day costs warm-up time for little gain. No study covers balancing several instruments; this is a design heuristic and should be labelled so in the UI ("guitar is behind this week").

Fit: `weekMinutes` in `hubs/progress/model.ts` already computes minutes per day; sessions carry `instrument`. A small `pickInstrument(weekSessions, split)` in `routine.ts` plus one Settings control.

### R6. Replace the XP challenge with one specific goal per day (S)

What: drop "Hit today's XP (100)". Use "Today's target": the focus block's next concrete offer from `advance` or the path ("two clean runs of the pentatonic box at 94 BPM", "confirm Open shapes from yesterday"). Keep the weak-spot reps and the streak. Show progress as evidence over time (tempo cleared, steps confirmed), with XP demoted to a footnote. Consider counting the streak in weeks with a minutes target, as Yousician does, since time available varies so much.

Why: Locke and Latham on specific proximal goals; the digest already found XP measured participation (R31). Lally: missed days do not break habits.

Fit: `dailyChallenges` in `rewards.ts` takes one more input (the target). `advance` already produces the plain-words label.

### R7. Small tempo-ladder and session tweaks (S each)

- **Start a returning day one step below the last cleared tempo**, then climb back. Overnight consolidation usually holds the gain (Walker), so this warms up rather than demotes. `advance` keys on setup and starts fresh, so this is a start-tempo rule in the drill shell.
- **An occasional stretch run**: after two passes at the goal, offer one run 10% faster before moving on (Allingham and Wöllner: musicians combine gradual increase with alternating fast and slow). Add it as an `AdvanceKind` offer; never required.
- **An away-from-instrument plan**: when the budget is short and no device is connected, offer ear, reading, circle and "listen and imagine": play a song section and follow it with the fingers away from the instrument (Driskell 1994; Bernardi 2013). The `none` slot already exists in `routine.ts`.

### R8. Paths the owner's goals need (structure only; content belongs to the instrument reports) (M to L)

- **Ukulele**: share guitar steps where the skill is the same, separate steps for re-entrant tuning.
- **Aerophone path**: the drills exist (fingers, breath and tongue), the path does not.
- **Reading path** across piano and the treble instruments, using the level climb.
- **A Songs step type**: `kind: "song"`, done when a section is played through at a target tempo on two days. Songs give no mastery by decision, but a path can still read run scores.

## 4. Session templates (for the owner to react to)

- **10 minutes, guitar day**: 3 min review (two due skills, checked), 7 min focus (path next step with the tempo ladder).
- **30 minutes, piano day**: 5 review (ear degrees, a due voicing), 12 focus (path step), 5 variety (scale in a new key), 8 song.
- **90 minutes, mixed**: 10 review incl. reading, 25 focus on the main instrument, 15 variety, 20 song, 10 jam, 10 second-instrument focus.
