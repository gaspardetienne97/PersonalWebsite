# Jazz and improvisation path, piano-led (round 2, 2026-10-27)

Builds on `context/research/curriculum-2026-10/piano.md` (Levine, Open Studio, Dobbins, standards order, ranked recommendations) and `context/research/piano-drills.md` (MIDI metrics, tempo tables, practice evidence). Those are not repeated here.

## 1. Target and scope

**Done means:** from a lead sheet the owner can play 40 or more standards in time with a band or backing track: state the head with a voiced left hand, comp rootless, quartal and block-chord voicings with real comping rhythms, improvise two or three choruses that follow the changes (guide-tone landings, bebop lines, pentatonic and motivic ideas), take a tune into a second key on request, play a ballad as solo piano, and reharmonise a simple standard. Repertoire spans the blues, swing standards, bossa, modal tunes, bebop heads, rhythm changes, ballads, Monk, Silver, Hancock and Evans.

**External benchmarks.** No single exam covers it, so three yardsticks:
- **Mark Levine, The Jazz Piano Book:** chapters on shells, left-hand and rootless voicings, tritone substitution, pentatonics, So What and fourth chords, upper structures, block chords, comping and reharmonisation all practised in 12 keys.
- **Open Studio piano pathway:** finish Level 3 ("know some tunes, can improvise a little") and enter Level 4 (two-handed voicings, block-chord soloing, solo piano).
- **Repertoire count:** a working book of 40 tunes from memory, which is what jam-session players and most teacher lists treat as "can sit in".

**Starting point.** Intermediate pianist who reads both clefs, knows triads and scales, and has met ii-V-I rootless voicings in the app. Level 1 assumes no jazz playing in time yet.

**Time at about 30 minutes a day.** 10 levels of 6 to 10 weeks each: about 20 months, range 16 to 26. Levels 1 to 3 go faster (6 to 7 weeks) because the owner already reads and has technique; levels 6, 9 and 10 are the long ones. A typical 30-minute day: 5 min voicings in keys, 5 min licks or ear, 15 min on the current tune ladder, 5 min free improvisation recorded. On 10-minute days do only the tune; on 60 to 90-minute days add transcription and a second shelf tune.

**The tune ladder (used at every level from 2 on).** Every shelf tune is learned through six rungs, taken from Open Studio's lead-sheet breakdown and Levine. The level says which rungs are required.

| Rung | Task | Done means |
|---|---|---|
| a | Roots in the left hand, melody in the right | Song Studio performance mode 90% at the shelf tempo, two days |
| b | Guide tones (3 and 7) through the form, then the melody on top | Two choruses, no wrong guide tone, at 80% of shelf tempo |
| c | Melody plus shells (1-3-7 or 1-7-3) | Two choruses 90% at shelf tempo |
| d | Rootless comping in time against a backing (no melody) | Two choruses, 90% correct voicings, on the comping rhythm |
| e | One chorus solo over the backing | Passes the level's improvisation metric (below) |
| f | The tune in a second key (down a 4th or the key a singer would ask for) | Rungs a and c in the new key, 85%, one day |

**How improvisation is graded.** Constraint games, as Levine and Open Studio teach them, each with a metric MIDI can see (the scoring is NEW, rank 3 in section 4): allowed-note share (games with a note set), chord tones on strong beats, 3rd or 7th landing within a beat of a chord change, rests (two-beat silences per chorus), rhythmic-motif repetition, and call and response windows. A self-check on a recording is the fallback until the scoring exists.

**Score sources.** Every MuseScore link below was found by searching musescore.com in the Orca browser and checking the result card (title, uploader, instrumentation, length, ratings), then opening the score page: all 169 linked pages loaded with the expected title on 2026-10-27 (one extra link, Gabe Raimondo's Nica's Dream, is checked from the search card only). "Lead sheet" means melody and chord symbols, the right input for the ladder. "Official" means a Hal Leonard or ArrangeMe licensed score (user 39593079 or 97106398): paid or Pro, and MusicXML export is not guaranteed. Community uploads vary in accuracy: check against the record. Fallbacks: the Hal Leonard Real Book (sixth edition) volumes, iReal Pro (the forum's "Jazz 1460" playlist covers every tune here), and lesson pages. I have not given Real Book page numbers because I could not verify them.

**App drill names** used below: `voicings` tabs (Major ii-V-I, Minor ii-V-i, Altered, Closest chords, Guide tones, Cadences), `comping` (blue-third Charleston patterns on C7, F7, G7), `licks` (31 licks, ii-V-I and minor), `chord-scales`, `jam` (ii-V-I, minor ii-V-i, 12-bar blues, modal vamp, plus pop), `scales` (piano with fingering, arpeggios), `two-hand` (incl. LH shell plus RH line), `ear` (progressions, chords-in-a-key, follow-the-bass, scales-and-modes, rhythm-dictation, sing-and-play-back), `rhythm`, `phrases`, `record`, `listening`, and Song Studio (import MusicXML or MIDI from MuseScore; wait mode, performance mode, fix-the-spot loops). NEW marks a drill the app lacks.

## 5. Open questions for the owner

1. Do you own a Real Book (Hal Leonard sixth edition) or iReal Pro? Several fallbacks below point there, and iReal charts could seed the tune player's changes instead of hand authoring.
2. MuseScore "official" Real Book scores (Hal Leonard, user 39593079) are paid or Pro-only and may not export MusicXML: are you fine with community lead sheets as the default and official ones only where no community score exists?
3. Which direction first after level 3: modal and Herbie (level 4) or bebop (level 5)? The order here is modal first because it is easier to improvise over; swapping 4 and 5 works.
4. Do you want solo piano (stride, ballads, arrangements) as a goal in itself, or mainly trio and band comping? That changes the weight of levels 7 and 10.
5. Do you have the recordings (streaming account, or owned files for slowing down)? The transcription steps assume you can loop and slow audio.
6. Is a jam partner or the Aerophone-over-piano recording loop realistic for call and response, or should the app's backing do all of it?
7. Tempo targets assume a weighted 88-key MIDI keyboard; is that what you play on?
