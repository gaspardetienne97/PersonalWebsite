# Finger drumming: technique, levels, repertoire (2026-10-13)

Local reference for the Pads build. Numbers marked "proposal" are not published by Melodics or anyone else.

## Teaching

- Layout: kick bottom-left, snare beside it, closed hat above the snare (Melodics' starting layout); no single standard ([Melodics](https://melodics.com/blog/getting-started-with-finger-drumming), [Quest for Groove](https://questforgroove.discourse.group/t/quest-for-groove-pad-layout/475)).
- Fingers: split hands for beginners (right hand eighth hats, left hand kick and snare; [Melodics](https://melodics.com/blog/the-3-universal-components-of-finger-drumming)); or one hand plays the beat and the other fills (Kroker, [NI](https://blog.native-instruments.com/how-to-master-the-art-of-finger-drumming-with-tim-kroker/); Mad Zach, [DJTT](https://djtechtools.com/2016/07/20/mad-zachs-techniques-learning-fingerdrumming-routine)). Quest for Groove learns stickings first, then spreads them over the kit.
- Posture: wrists up, fingers curved, strike with the tips and bounce off (rebound). Two-finger linear: index, middle, or both together (fastest).
- Velocity: turn Full Level off on the MPC; 16 Levels is a velocity ladder.

## Technique order

Singles (one pad, then alternating; a 1-2-3-4 pyramid), doubles and two-finger rolls on one pad, three-finger rolls, the paradiddle family and six-stroke roll and flam ([PAS 40](https://pas.org/rudiments/)), sticking permutations spread over the kit, accents and ghosts (moving accent in a sixteenth stream), independence (hats fixed while the kick varies), fills across pads, Note Repeat and trap rolls (1/32, 1/16T; [MPC forum](https://www.mpc-forums.com/viewtopic.php?p=1825413)), chops, swing by hand.

## Levels

- Melodics: 15 grades and stages, tolerances tighten, timing precision per note, streaks, Auto BPM +10 per pass ([stages](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15091931-stages), [scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring), [practice](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/6777027-practice-mode)). Windows and BPM per grade not published.
- Evidence: noticeable timing difference about 2.5% of a beat; pro drummers 1-10 ms off a click, each limb with its own bias (Fujii et al. 2011).
- Proposal, perfect / good: beginner ±35 / ±70 ms; intermediate ±25 / ±50; advanced ±15 / ±35; good capped at 40% of a sixteenth. Pass: 90% good, 70% perfect.
- Proposal, tempo bands: foundations 70-90, grooves 85-100, house 120-128, trap 130-150 (half time), drum and bass 170-175.

## Repertoire (grids from [Ethan Hein's transcriptions](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/my-collection-of-transcribed-rhythm-patterns))

Basic rock, four on the floor, boom bap A and B, Funky Drummer (~97-100, ghosted snare), Impeach the President (~95), When the Levee Breaks (~70), The Big Beat, Amen (~138, four bars), Apache, trap (140, 1/32 and 1/16T rolls), drum and bass two-step (172), dembow (90-100), Dilla feel (kick and hats straight, snare early, swing). Think not verified; afrobeats low confidence. Patterns as grids are fine; recordings are not shipped.

MPC swing (Roger Linn): delays every second sixteenth; 50 straight, 66 triplet; steps 50 54 58 60 62 66 70 75 ([Attack](https://attackmagazine.com/features/interview/roger-linn-swing-groove-magic-mpc-timing/2)).

## Feedback

Per-voice bias and jitter; velocity separation and band share for accents and ghosts; swing ratio as an MPC % (target ± 2 to 3 points); steadiness (4 bars of click, 8 silent; drift in BPM and offset when it ends); tempo ladder (+5 or +10 after a pass, fall back after two misses).

## Path proposal

Pulse and steadiness; singles; backbeat split; doubles and rolls; paradiddles; accents and ghosts; boom bap, Impeach, Levee; independence; fills; swing (56, 62); house and trap; the breaks; from memory without a click.

## Added later: Think, afrobeats, Tony Allen

- Think (Lyn Collins, 1972), the "Yeah! Woo!" break: one transcription found ([Ethan Hein, 2026](https://www.ethanhein.com/wp/2026/it-takes-two/), notation and annotated waveform). Kick on 1; snare 5 and 13 with ghosts on 8 and 11; sixteenths are a tambourine behind the beat, no hi-hat; about 113 BPM ([SongBPM](https://songbpm.com/@lyn-collins/think-about-it)).
- Afrobeats: no canonical transcription; production sources disagree ([BeatCraft](https://beatcraft.io/drum-patterns), [Songen](https://songen.app/blog/how-to-make-afrobeats-drums/), [BeatsToRapOn](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/afrobeats-production-guide-rhythm-chords-mixing/)). Shared: kick on 1 with syncopated pushes (7, 11), rim or clap answering on 4 and 7 (3-3-2), woody sounds, busy shaker sixteenths, light swing, 95 to 115 BPM. The app uses BeatCraft's version, flagged as one common version.
- Tony Allen's first afrobeat pattern ([Joe the Drummer](https://joethedrummer.com/this-is-the-first-afrobeat-drum-groove-according-to-tony-allen/), from Allen's own demonstration; kicks in double strokes per [Modern Drummer](https://moderndrummer.com/2011/10/tony-allen-afrobeat-innovator)). No tempo documented.

## Added later: Latin, Caribbean and Brazilian grooves

- Reggaeton: the dembow, kick on every beat, snare 3+3+2 on the a of 1, the & of 2, the a of 3, the & of 4 (MusicRadar, Native Instruments). Full version adds a low tom on 1 and 3. 85-100 BPM.
- Tamborzão (funk carioca): DJ Luciano Oliveira's reduction "tum pa-pá pum pá"; drum-machine kick, toms and a slap conga, no hats (Caceres, Ferrari and Palombini 2014, "A era do Tamborzão"). Around 130 BPM; funk 150 from 2016, with DJ Sany Pitbull's extra kick.
- Kompa: cowbell `X..XX...X.X.X...`, hats, gong (floor tom) on 4. One forum source only; kick part and tempo not documented. Shipped as provisional.
- Reggae one drop, steppers, modern dancehall: Modern Drummer and DRUM! lessons. One drop is half-time with kick and cross-stick on 3, often shuffled.
- Soca: four on the floor, snare 3+3+2, open hat on the &. Teachers disagree on the hats; the app uses Boston Drum Lessons' version.
- Zouk: sixteenth hats loud/soft, tibwa cinquillo on the rim, snare on 2 and 4.
- Cumbia: guacharaca on the hats, llamador on 2 and 4; kick placement varies by source (every beat, 2 and 4, or tumbao); the app keeps it on the beat.
- Bossa nova: surdo kick with pushes, bossa clave on the cross-stick over two bars. Samba: surdo figure on the kick, pedal hat on the &s, tempo approximate.
- Merengue skipped: no reliable pad grid found (the tambora part does not reduce well to kit pads).
