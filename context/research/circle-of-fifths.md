# Circle of fifths: teaching, memorising, applying (2026-10-13)

Local reference for the circle-of-fifths reference section, drill and path. Timing targets are a synthesis, not measured norms.

## Uses, most useful first

1. Key signatures: clockwise adds a sharp, counter-clockwise a flat; FCGDAEB forwards for sharps, backwards for flats ([OMT 1.10](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Book%3A_Open_Music_Theory/01%3A_Fundamentals/1.10%3A_Key_Signatures)).
2. Relative minors on the inner ring, a minor third below ([Schmidt-Jones 6.07](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Understanding_Basic_Music_Theory_(Schmidt-Jones)/06%3A_Notes_and_Scales/6.07%3A_The_Circle_of_Fifths)).
3. Root motion by fourths (ii-V-I, turnarounds, secondary dominants): "Most chord movement within tunes follows portions of the cycle" (Levine, [sample](https://www.jazzbooks.com/mm5/samples/D-MLT.pdf)).
4. All 12 keys in cycle order; jazz players go counter-clockwise (C F Bb Eb...) because it follows ii-V-I roots (Levine).
5. Closely related keys and modulation ([Mount 1.10](https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Fundamentals_Function_and_Form_(Mount)/01%3A_Fundamentals/1.10%3A_The_Circle_of_Fifths)).
6. A key's chords: I with its neighbours (IV, V), their inner ring (ii, vi, iii); vii° needs its own slot.
7. Transposition. 8. Tritone substitution directly across. 9. Modes by brightness (white-note modes start F C G D A E B). 10. Enharmonic keys at the bottom.

## Memorising

- FCGDAEB "Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle"; flats reversed (BEAD-GCF). Staff shapes: sharps down four, up five (except A#); flats up four, down five ([LearnMusicTheory.net](https://learnmusictheory.net/PDFs/pdffiles/01-02-05-KeySignaturesSelfStudyTips.pdf)).
- Sharp keys: a half step above the last sharp. Flat keys: the second-to-last flat. C and F memorised ([teoria](https://www.teoria.com/en/reference/j-k/key-sig-id.php)).
- Counts add to 7 for a letter and its flat (D 2 sharps, Db 5 flats).
- The goal is recall, not derivation; drill both directions, small groups, major and minor separately first.
- Introduce by accidental count on both sides (C; G, F; D, Bb; ...), as graded syllabi do.

## Drills and criteria

- Signature to key (major and minor), key to count, next/previous, closely related keys, order of sharps and flats, a key's chords, a blank wheel, the cycle on the instrument.
- Courses: 20 in a row on musictheory.net key-signature reading (AP summer work); timed fluency quizzes (UVM).
- Targets (synthesis): recall facts under 2 s; staff signature under 3 s; a key's chords under 5 s; 95% at speed over ~20 items on two days. Track latency per key; B, F#/Gb, C#/Db and flat-side minors lag.
- Interleave (Taylor and Rohrer 2010: 77% vs 38% next day); space over days ([Cepeda 2008](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/CVRWP2008.html)); learners prefer blocked practice, so show progress.

## Sequence

1. Concept and the near keys (C G F D Bb). 2. Order of sharps and flats. 3. Key and count by bands (0-1, 2-4, 5-7 and enharmonics), then interleaved. 4. Signatures on the staff, treble then bass. 5. Relative minors. 6. Chords from the wedge, closely related keys. 7. Applied: piano scales clockwise from C (C G D A E share a fingering); jazz ii-V-I counter-clockwise; guitar chords round the fourths; transpose riffs round the cycle. 8. Mix cycle order with random order. 9. Name the key before reading.

## Visualisation

Majors outside with enharmonic pairs at the bottom; relative minors inside; a diminished ring; counts on segments; the picked key's wedge with numerals; audio on tap; tritone opposite. Pitfalls: unclear direction, missing vii°, wrong spelling, relative vs parallel minor, every label at once, the wheel as a crutch (hide it in timed drills; test with a blank wheel).
