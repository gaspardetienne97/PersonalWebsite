# Aural-skills research: drills A to H, sequencing and path placement (2026-10-12)

Research done for the ear-training expansion (rhythm by ear, scales and modes, minor keys and sevenths, singing back, inversions, bass line, error detection, fine pitch). Local reference only.

## Sequencing anchors

- **EarMaster suggested order** ([forum, manual excerpt](https://www.earmaster.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=6970)): (1) Interval Comparison and Rhythm Imitation, (2) Interval ID, Melody Imitation and Rhythmic Sight-Reading, (3) Chord ID, Scale ID and Rhythmic Dictation, (4) the rest. Rhythm activities 1 to 8 bars by default ([SOS review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/earmaster)); first Tutor lessons at 90 bpm.
- **Karpinski, Manual for Ear Training and Sight Singing** ([TOC](https://librarycatalog.ecu.edu/catalog/6340230)): meter/rhythm (1), error detection (4), dictation (10), ties and dotted beat (12), compound (16), minor (17), triplets (20), sixteenths (22), dominant triad (25), syncopation (31), cadences (34), bass-line dictation (36) before root/first-inversion triads (37), triad qualities (39), vii° (40), ii (41), vi (42), iii (43), V7 (44), six-four (46), other sevenths (47), modes (49 to 50).
- **Sierra College, Equipping the Musical Ear** ([TOC](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Sierra_College/Equipping_the_Musical_Ear)): simple meter, rhythmic dictation, compound, minor keys, intervals, beat divisions, triads, cadences, sevenths, modes, syncopation, harmonic dictation, six-four.

## A. Rhythm by ear (echo and dictation)

- Echo: pattern over a count-in; clap, tap or MIDI back in time; per-onset timing markers. Latency compensation matters (EarMaster 5 default 46 ms).
- Dictation: build the answer from note-value blocks or a step grid; rests, ties, swing, tuplets as settings.
- No published tap window found. Suggested: about ±80 ms (capped at 25% of the smallest value) for beginners, tightening to ±40 ms; tappers lean early ([Repp 2005](https://academic.oup.com/book/3298/chapter/144308310)).
- Levels: L1 quarter + eighth pair (1 bar 4/4); L2 + quarter rest, half note (2 bars); L3 dotted quarter-eighth, ties; L4 6/8; L5 sixteenth groupings; L6 eighth-quarter-eighth syncopation, triplets; L7 dotted eighth-sixteenth, sixteenth syncopation, swing.
- Echo is a day-one skill; dictation comes after melody imitation.

## B. Scales and modes

- Hear the scale, pick its name ([ToneGym](https://www.tonegym.co/exercise/scales): direction, root, speed, at least 3 choices; streak of 10 before adding).
- Auralia levels ([syllabus](https://download.risingsoftware.com/doc/syllabus/auralia_solfege_fixeddo.pdf)): major + harmonic minor; + melodic minor; + aeolian; + chromatic, whole tone; + pentatonics, blues; + dorian, phrygian; + lydian, mixolydian; + locrian.
- Modes heard parallel on one tonic, each one note from major or minor, by brightness ([Hein](https://ethanhein.com/wp/2021/modes-from-light-to-dark-around-the-chromatic-circle/)).
- Recommended for this app: major vs natural minor; harmonic minor; pentatonics and blues; melodic minor; Mixolydian and Dorian; Lydian and Phrygian; Locrian. Advanced answer: which degree changed.
- After intervals and minor tonality.

## C. Minor keys and sevenths for chords in a key and progressions

- Minor: i-iv-V-i with a harmonic-minor V; then VI and ii°; then III and VII (subtonic) contrasted with vii°.
- Sevenths: V7 first, then ii7 (iiø7 in minor), then Imaj7/IVmaj7, then vi7, vii°7.
- After major-key chords and after minor tonality.

## D. Singing back

- Auralia interval singing: P4/P5/P8 ascending; then both directions; + M2/M3; + m2/m3/TT; + 6ths; + 7ths. Sight-singing L1 is 4 notes on degrees 1 to 3.
- EarMaster ([forum](https://earmaster.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=15307)): ±50 cents averaged over the note, interval notes held 1600 ms, any octave, no octave jumps inside a melody.
- 50 cents is the research cutoff for "in tune" ([Berkowska and Dalla Bella 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00714/full)).
- Suggested: ±50 cents tightening to ±25; ignore octave; stable 300 to 500 ms.

## E. Chord inversions

- Root / 1st / 2nd (/ 3rd for sevenths). Auralia: root position as arpeggio; all inversions as arpeggio; block; dom7 root; dom7 inversions arpeggio then block.
- Root vs 1st first, then 2nd; sevenths last. After bass-line work (Karpinski 36 then 37).

## F. Bass line dictation

- 3 to 5 chords; enter bass degrees, then derive numerals ([College Board](https://secure-media.collegeboard.org/apc/2001_qa_music_10529.pdf): bass first, then chords, then soprano; [Chenette, MTO](https://mtosmt.org/ojs/index.php/mto/article/view/613/149)).
- Root-position basses on 1, 4, 5; then 2 and 6; then stepwise inversion basses (1-7-1, 3-4-5).
- After chords in a key, before inversions and progressions.

## G. Error detection

- Show the written melody (or degrees), play it with one note changed, tap the wrong note. Knobs: 0 or 1 errors ("no error" answer), step vs leap, diatonic vs chromatic, pitch vs rhythm, 4 to 8 notes.
- Karpinski ch. 4, before dictation; recognition rather than recall.

## H. Fine pitch discrimination

- Auralia Tuning: ±40 to ±10 cents; compare, identify, match ([Rising](https://www.risingsoftware.com/news/tuning)).
- 2-down/1-up staircase (Levitt 1971, ~71%). Best JND about 3 cents near 2 kHz; non-musicians reach musician levels after 4 to 8 h of training ([Micheyl et al. 2006](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/influence-of-musical-and-psychoacoustical-training-on-pitch-discr/)).
- Ladder: 50, 35, 25, 18, 12, 8, 5 cents, complex tones; higher/lower first, then sharp/flat against a reference.
- Coarse levels support singing; fine tuning is a side skill.

## Proposed Ear Path

1 Anchor the tonic · 2 Diatonic degrees · 3 Sing or play degrees back · **4 Echo a rhythm** · **5 Spot the wrong note** · 6 Melody dictation · **7 Rhythm dictation** · 8 Chromatic color · 9 Hear interval size · 10 Name intervals · **11 Sing intervals** · **12 Scales and modes** · 13 Mixed review · 14 Chords in a key (major then minor) · **15 Follow the bass** · **16 Inversions by ear** · 17 Progressions (minor, V7, sevenths). Fine pitch past 25 cents is an optional Tuning drill.

## Other paths

- MPC: rhythm echo L1 after "two pads on click"; echo sixteenths after "sixteenths"; drum-groove dictation on the 16-step grid after "two-bar boom bap"; rhythm error detection before "four-bar piece with fill".
- Guitar: chord quality at "open chords"; strum echo before "island strum"; pentatonic and blues scale ID at "minor pentatonic box"; play back a heard lick before "lick in new key".
- Piano: inversions by ear beside "triads and inversions"; bass and progressions beside "I-IV-V-I"; blues scale and dom7 at "blues lick over C7"; error detection before "sight-read a phrase".
- Jazz: seventh qualities at "chord symbols"; modes by ear at "chord-scales"; ii7-V7-Imaj7 and iiø7-V7-i at "rootless voicings"; swing echo at "licks"; sing back a lick before "improvise".

## Completion criteria

- 66% is fine with 4 or more choices; use 80% with 2 or 3 choices. At least 20 unaided items and every item in the active set.
- Rhythm echo: 80% of items with every onset in the window and no extra or missing taps.
- Dictation: per-note accuracy 75% or more and half the items fully right.
- Singing: 75% of notes held stably in the cents window.
- Fine pitch: staircase threshold at or below 25 cents for the path link.

Gaps: Complete Ear Trainer's chapter list and Tonedear specifics not found; tap windows, mic hold time and cent ladders are design suggestions, not published values.
