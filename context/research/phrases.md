# Popular short phrases per instrument

Research for the Phrases drill (2026-10-13). The rule is public domain only:
- traditional tunes;
- composers who died more than 70 years ago;
- generic vocabulary of a style.

No copyrighted songs or recordings.

## Curricula consulted

- **Essential Elements for Band Book 1** (sax): Hot Cross Buns and Lightly Row early, starting from B, A and G.
- **Roland Aerophone lesson app:** 12 fingering lessons plus guide-melody songs. The song list isn't public.
- **Melodics finger drumming:** grooves, independence and fills.
- **JustinGuitar:** minor pentatonic Pattern 1 at fret 5, and five blues licks from it.
- **Bluegrass:** the G run is the most-played run.

## Decisions

- **Chords:** a chord grader (scoring/phrases) treats notes that start together as one step, so the turnaround, double-stop intro, boom-chuck, boogie shuffle, island strum and Hawaiian vamp are in, with piano chord phrases (I-vi-IV-V, Pachelbel's Canon chords, a left-hand shuffle).
- **MPC pads:** phrase material is short cells, not the long grooves. They were added to the groove repertoire under a "phrases" topic:
  - clave cells
  - tresillo
  - paradiddle split
  - linear 3 over 4
  - one-beat fill
  - shave-and-a-haircut tag
  - snare build
  - stomp-stomp-clap

  The "In the Air Tonight" style tom fill is a performance, so it isn't included; "down the toms" covers it.
- **Aerophone:** the owner asked for saxophone and flute material. A second pass read the scores and kept 10 public-domain excerpts plus 4 vocabulary licks.
  - Scores read:
    - IMSLP: Morning Mood #40004, Badinerie #190451, Gluck #262453, Bumblebee #99876, the L'Arlésienne sax part #50533, Boléro #299525.
    - Wikipedia LilyPond: the L'Arlésienne Menuet, The Old Castle.
    - Mutopia: The Entertainer, Maple Leaf Rag.
  - Left out because they need 32nd notes or triplets: Syrinx, Dance of the Reed Flutes, the Carmen Entr'acte.
  - Left out because the notes couldn't be checked: Queen of the Night.
  - Left out because they are copyrighted: the method books (Rubank, Essential Elements). Their early tunes are traditional and stay at level 1.
